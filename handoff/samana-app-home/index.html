Enterprise

The whole business, on one page.

Every job, run and season your company has, rolled into one picture of the money. Flow takes what the plan says will happen and lays it out by month: what comes in, what goes out, when the most cash is needed and what is left. Each business unit on its own, or all of them together.

Flow

The cash, by month, before you spend it.

Flow follows every change, so you see a job get bigger or smaller and when the money moves. Material and labor are split on every line, and every dollar carries a cost code that goes straight to the books.

  • Cash by month, and the most cash you will need
  • Every revision and how far it moved the money
  • Business units side by side, or added up
  • The owner's view: margins, the profit view, who has access
Manufacturing

Lean production, from a plan, not a whiteboard.

Most shops run the floor on spreadsheets, a whiteboard and somebody's memory. Lean puts the run on one plan: every step in order, the people it needs, the rate it should hit and the parts it uses. When the floor falls behind, you see it the same day, not at the end of the month. The same Lean runs a farm's season.

Less waiting.

Each step starts when the one before it is done and the people are free. Samana flags the day two steps want the same person.

Less waste.

Order what the plan uses, when it uses it. No rush freight, no shelf of parts nobody needed.

Nothing done twice.

Every change is a revision, kept exactly as sent. Everyone works from the issued plan, not a half-finished edit.

Lean · the plan

Steps, people and dates, worked out for you.

Put in the steps of a run and how many people each takes. Lean lays them on the calendar Monday to Friday, keeps them inside the window the run has to finish in, and moves everything after a step when that step moves.

  • Drag a start and the steps after it follow
  • People each day, with the busiest day and who is double-booked
  • Go puts a step on the crew's list; Verify closes it once it is checked
Lean · rates and hours

Planned rate against real rate, on every step.

Each step carries the rate it should run at: units an hour, pieces a person-hour. Log what the floor really did and Samana draws it under the plan, green at or above, red below. Hours by week sit against the limit, so overtime shows up before it is paid.

  • Rates in the step's own units
  • Actuals against plan, as soon as days are logged
  • Hours by week with the line you set
Lean farming

The same plan runs a growing season.

Field prep, planting, harvest and drying are steps like any other. Lean hands them to the crew's phones in Farmmetric, our compliance app for licensed growers, and the crew checks them off in the field.

Construction

Agile design-build. Draw it, price it, start it.

A fit-out, a new line, a renovation or a whole building. Put in the sizes the way you measure them and Samana draws it to scale and builds the takeoff behind it: every piece with material, labor and time. Change the design and the price changes with it, so you can go from first sketch to a priced, scheduled start without redrawing anything. Recorded on the sample job every account starts with.

  1. DrawnRooms, walls, equipment, to scale
  2. CountedEvery piece, hour and price in the takeoff
  3. CodedMaterial and labor, by division and cost code
  4. In FlowDeposits in, materials and labor out, by month
Design changes

Change the design. See what it costs, right away.

Publish a revision and it is kept exactly as sent, costs frozen. Change the design and Samana shows both side by side, division by division, up or down, and Flow moves the cash to match. The crew opens the issued revision, not your half-finished edits.

  • Rev A, B, C, with what changed and what it cost
  • Every rev carried into Flow
  • Step-by-step work orders with check-offs from the field
Resource planning

See it. Plan it. Track it. Decide better every year.

People, hours, materials and money are your resources. Samana puts them in one place so the decisions for this year, and every year after, come from your own numbers instead of a guess.

01

See your resources

Who you have, what they are on, what is in stock and what the cash looks like, across every job, run and season.

02

Plan your resources

Put each person on the step that fits them, line up the partners and trades around them, and order what the plan uses before you run short.

03

Track your resources

Hours, rates, materials and dollars as they really happened, logged against the plan as the work gets done.

Year after year.

This year's real numbers become next year's starting point. Compare what you planned with what happened, adjust the budget where it was off, and every season gets tighter than the one before.

  1. PlanThe budget and the steps for the year
  2. TrackThe actuals, as the work is done
  3. ComparePlanned against real, last year against this
  4. AdjustNext year's budget, from your own numbers
